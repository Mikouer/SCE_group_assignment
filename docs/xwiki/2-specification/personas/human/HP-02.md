## Specific capabilities and needs

While awake, Cleber can hear and understand Echo, including a prompt from a
speaker in another room. He can answer a simple question and say "not now" or
"I'm done". He can independently walk to the kitchen, heat a meal and eat.

Dementia can cause him to forget a meal or an earlier reminder. He may sleep
through a prompt, so silence does not establish refusal or that he heard it.

## Values and motivations

Security ([HV-01](../../../1-foundation/human-factors/human-values/HV-01.md)) and
achievement/independence ([HV-02](../../../1-foundation/human-factors/human-values/HV-02.md))
have very high priority. Health ([HV-04](../../../1-foundation/human-factors/human-values/HV-04.md)) also matters.
He wants support that preserves his ability to choose and act independently.

## Social network and relationships

Cleber lives alone in an apartment. Sarah visits every two to three days to
prepare meals and check on him. His grandson Gilbert lives in another city.

## History and experiences

Alarms and notes have failed when he was in another room or asleep, each
reported two to three times a week in [SA-01](../../../1-foundation/operational-demands/activities/SA-01.md).
Prepared meals have been found partly eaten or untouched.

## Constraints and privacy considerations

Not eating may reflect forgetting, sleep or a choice: he may not be hungry or
may dislike the prepared food ([PS-01](../../../1-foundation/operational-demands/problem-scenarios/PS-01.md)).
Reminders need to respect that choice without making him feel watched.
