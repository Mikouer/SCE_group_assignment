## Method

| Part | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Evaluators | Two evaluators from other course groups (groups 4 and 7), each in a separate session on build B1. Between them they completed 26 scenario runs of Path A (12 and 14 runs, seven different scenarios each). |
| Roles | A facilitator introduces the Use Case, its objective and the persona, shows the artifact one step at a time, and takes notes without intervening or explaining problems away. An evaluator steps through the Action Sequence as the persona, answers the questions, justifies the answers and hands the notes to the facilitator. |
| Personas | Path A: Cleber ([HP-02](../../2-specification/personas/human/HP-02.md)), who receives the reminders. Path B: Sarah ([HP-01](../../2-specification/personas/human/HP-01.md)), who receives the alert. Path B was offered, but nobody played Sarah: her step and open questions were left unanswered. |
| Steps walked | Path A: UC01 step 2 (the spoken reminder), and steps 4 and 5 (responding, postponing at reminder 1 or 2, the follow-up reminders, at most five in a scenario), and step 7 (ending). Path B: steps 6 and 7 (the unresolved-meal alert and the "resolved" update) and the visit summary of step 9 as shown on Sarah's phone. Steps 1, 3 and 8 happen inside the system and are not walkable; the log behind them is checked against the M-04 rules in the simulation, not judged by the evaluator. The scenarios to walk (twelve for Cleber, four for Sarah) are listed in a side panel of the prototype. |
| Questions at each step | The four baseline questions of [EM-04](../../1-foundation/human-factors/evaluation-methods/EM-04.md) (right effect, action noticed, action associated with the effect, progress seen) are tailored to each step, and only those that apply are asked. Each is Yes/No, required, and justified. A step's questions are asked in full the first time that step appears in the session; when it appears again (another reminder, another scenario) the evaluator only notes whether anything was different this time. The same applies to Sarah's open questions after a second and later alert. **Step 2, initial reminder:** Does the reminder encourage Cleber to consider lunch without sounding demanding? Would Cleber notice and understand it in the described situation? Is the next action clear while leaving him free to choose? **Step 4, postponement:** Does Echo accept the postponement without making it feel like a mistake? Is it clear what happens next? Does Cleber retain a meaningful sense of choice? **Step 4, entering the kitchen:** Does Echo's quiet behaviour let Cleber continue independently? Can Cleber eat without being required to say "I'm done"? **Step 4, repeated reminder:** Is it clear that the reminder concerns the same planned meal? Does it still feel like an invitation rather than pressure? Is its purpose understandable? **Step 5, third reminder:** Is this reminder as gentle as the earlier ones, with no louder or more pressing wording? Does Cleber still have a meaningful choice about responding? **Step 7, ending:** if eating is confirmed, is it clear the interaction has ended and reminders will stop? If not, is it clear the sequence has ended for now? **Path B, step 6, alert:** Does the alert tell Sarah which meal is unresolved and why? Is it clear what she can do next while she stays free to choose? Does it read as a situation worth an alert rather than an alarm? **Path B, step 7, "resolved" update:** Is it clear the meal is resolved and nothing more is needed? Does the update stay calm? |
| Recording | Per step: the answers and justifications to that step's questions, which carry the step-level success and failure stories. Once at the end: open questions that collect the success stories, failure stories, design suggestions and problems outside the sequence (see the follow-up row), because the evaluators do not write during the session. The prototype also saves each scenario's event log and its M-04 conformance result. The evaluator hands the notes to the facilitator. |
| Follow-up questions (CL3 and task progress) | Open-ended, after the walkthrough: (1) Was there a moment when a reminder felt like too much, or pushy? Which one? (2) Did it feel possible to say no or to postpone, and was it clear how? (3) Would you want Echo to stay quiet at any point? When? Answers to (1) to (3) are coded as pressure, confusion, none or other, as in [M-03](../../1-foundation/human-factors/measures/M-03.md). (4) Were you able to recognise how the reminders and the escalation to Sarah help build a healthy eating habit and support Cleber's meals? Why or why not? (5) Which moments worked well for Cleber, and what made them work? (6) Where could it go wrong for Cleber in real life, for example if he is asleep, confused or irritated? What would happen? (7) What would you change about what Echo says, when it speaks, or what happens next? (8) Is there anything that would matter to Cleber that these scenarios did not cover? Questions (5) to (8) are the findings of the standard form (success stories, failure stories, design suggestions, other problems) asked in the use case's own words. Question (4) is the fourth baseline question (will the user see that progress is being made toward the task) asked once about the whole walkthrough; it is not coded for CL3. |
| Open questions for CL4 and CL5 | Not answered in this walkthrough (see Personas). Planned for the person playing Sarah, in her own words, no scores. After the alert: (A1) What did the alert tell you: which meal, how long, why unresolved? (A2) What would you do next, and why? (A3) Was anything unclear or missing? After the visit summary: (B1) Which meals were eaten and which were not? (B2) Was the reminder timing and postponement information understandable? (B3) Did the plate-away times help? (B4) Was missing data clearly shown, or could a gap be read as "all fine"? After the summary she also answers: (C1) Which parts of the alerts and the summary worked well for you as Sarah, and what made them work? (C2) What would you change so they are easier to act on, or is there anything these scenarios did not cover that would matter to you? Her answer to A1 is stored with the real reason for the alert, so the facilitator can compare. These questions are an addition to [M-05](../../1-foundation/human-factors/measures/M-05.md), whose Likert items are not used in this formative cycle. |
| Follow-up check on B1.1 | The log and summary were not walked on B1. After build B1.1, the team ran the scripted scenarios and read the result of the prototype's own M-04 conformance check on each run, and a team member went through Sarah's visit summary on the phone view and compared it with the criteria of PR4. No participants took part, and the prototype supplies the meal states, so no real sensing is involved. |
| Material | The prototype [AT-01](../artifacts/AT-01.md), a persona sheet for each path, the Action Sequence and task list, the recording form and the follow-up questions. |

## Formative-only fields

| Field | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Finding(s) | **PR1, initial reminder:** both evaluators answered yes to every question about the initial reminder. One wrote that it is "framed as a question rather than a command". **PR2, bounded reminders and postponement:** yes throughout for the postponement, the repeated reminder and the third reminder. **PR3, escalation and closure:** both answered yes to Cleber's ending step. Sarah's alert, her responses and the notification of Gilbert were not walked. **PR4, log and report:** not walked in the walkthrough, and the plate and sensor rules were not exercised in B1. In the follow-up check on B1.1 the prototype's own conformance check passed on the log and the summary worked as specified when inspected. Plate leaving and returning, offline-sensor data gaps and the one-hour attention rule depend on real sensing and were not exercised with real events. **Conformance check ([M-04](../../1-foundation/human-factors/measures/M-04.md)):** across the 26 runs the prototype's check recorded 186 passes, no failures and 152 not-applicable results. **CL3, perceived autonomy:** neither evaluator reported a reminder as pressure or too much ("the reminders were good and quite non-pressuring"; "all were reminders were optional"). Both found it possible to say no or postpone. **CL4, CL5:** no findings, because Sarah's questions were not answered. **Unexpected findings (outside the stated Premises and Claims):** (1) a spoken prompt may scare or confuse Cleber, for example when he is asleep or not expecting a voice; one evaluator suggested a gentler start, such as a visible light that can be traced to Echo's voice. (2) After "not now", evaluators wanted to know when Echo would speak again and suggested choosing the delay ("not now, but in 30 minutes"). (3) An empty plate may not mean the food was eaten: if Cleber throws the food away, Echo might see an empty plate and consider it eaten. (4) Suggestions for a short check-in or small talk at the start and a "how was the food, are you done?" exchange at the end. |
| Implications | A chime and a light now precede each spoken reminder, and Echo announces the 30-minute postponement delay (both in build B1.1 and in the Specification: [UC01](../../2-specification/use-cases/uc01/UC01.md), [IDP-01](../../2-specification/interaction-design-patterns/IDP-01.md), [RP-01](../../2-specification/personas/robot/RP-01.md)). Because an empty plate does not show eating, eating is confirmed only from observed consumption, and [UC01-F2-PR5](../../2-specification/use-cases/uc01/UC01-F2-PR5.md) was added to be checked against ground truth ([M-06](../../1-foundation/human-factors/measures/M-06.md)). The suggestions for small talk and a post-meal exchange are deferred to avoid burden. Sleep interruption and discarded food remain unresolved. What the walkthrough could not show goes to the summative test ([ER-02](ER-02.md)): Sarah's alert and summary, the log, real sensing and timing, and measured responses for CL1 to CL5. |

## Evidence produced

One Evidence entry each for PR1 ([EV-01](../evidence/EV-01.md)), PR2 ([EV-02](../evidence/EV-02.md)), PR3 ([EV-03](../evidence/EV-03.md)), CL3 ([EV-04](../evidence/EV-04.md)) and, from the B1.1 follow-up check, PR4 ([EV-05](../evidence/EV-05.md)). Each states its basis and limitations: two evaluators who only imagine the persona (which is hard for someone with dementia), a small sample in which every step answer was yes, and a prototype that is not a working system. EV-05 was checked by the team that built the prototype, not by independent evaluators.
