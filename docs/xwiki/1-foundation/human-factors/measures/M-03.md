# M-03: Perceived autonomy

| Field | Description |
| --- | --- |
| ID | M-03 |
| Name | Perceived autonomy |
| Status | In Progress |
| Evidence basis | Bespoke short questionnaire, grounded in self-determination theory (Ryan and Deci, 2000, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68); the theory behind [HV-02](../human-values/HV-02.md) and [HFC-02](../concepts/HFC-02.md)) and in findings on agency in people with dementia (Dixon, Piper and Lazar, 2021, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3411764.3445225)). The items and the 1 to 5 scale are written for this project and are not a validated instrument. |
| Construct measured | Cleber's perceived choice and control while receiving eating-related support from Echo, including his comfort with being reminded again. |
| Type | Quantitative (Likert), with one optional open comment. |
| Instrument or procedure | A five-item questionnaire read aloud by the facilitator after the meal-support session, answered on a 1 to 5 scale (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree): (1) I felt I could decide for myself how to respond to Echo. (2) Echo's reminders felt like an invitation, not an order. (3) I did not feel pressured to eat when Echo spoke. (4) I felt in control of what happened during the reminders. (5) I would be comfortable with Echo reminding me like this again. Then one open question: "Was there a moment when a reminder felt like too much?" The questions are read aloud and kept short so that they remain suitable for a future session with a person with dementia. |
| Scoring or coding scheme | Mean of the five items (1 to 5); higher means greater perceived autonomy. Item scores are also reported, because item 5 (acceptance) can differ from items 1 to 4. The open comment is coded as *pressure*, *confusion*, *none* or *other*. **Proposed target: mean of at least 4**, following the target used in the manual's worked autonomy example. |
| Validity and reliability | Provisional. Adapted for this project and not validated; [HFC-02](../concepts/HFC-02.md) supports the relevance of agency but does not validate the questionnaire. Residents with dementia may find a questionnaire hard to complete: in a study of a self-report quality-of-life scale (Logsdon et al., 2002, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/00006842-200205000-00016)), 155 of 177 patients could complete it, and those who could not had low cognitive scores. The questionnaire is therefore read aloud and complemented by the open comment and by observation of avoidance. |
| Serves | Validation. |
| Burden | About two minutes per resident, but moderate overall: residents with dementia need consent, accessible administration and attention to cognitive load. |

Used by [EM-02](../evaluation-methods/EM-02.md) to examine [OBJ-02](../../../2-specification/use-cases/OBJ-02.md) and
[UC01-F2-CL3](../../../2-specification/use-cases/uc01/UC01-F2-CL3.md). The questionnaire should be piloted on one colleague for
comprehension before the session.

The open question was used as the follow-up for [UC01-F2-CL3](../../../2-specification/use-cases/uc01/UC01-F2-CL3.md) in the cognitive walkthrough ([EM-04](../evaluation-methods/EM-04.md)). The Likert items are planned for [ER-02](../../../3-evaluation/reports/ER-02.md) (Session 2).
